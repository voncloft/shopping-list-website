Hungarian Beef Goulash
High pressure 35 minutes; wait 15 minutes before releasing steam.
Use a 6- or 8-quart Instant Pot. Before closing the lid, keep the food and liquid below the PC MAX / 2/3 line. This full batch is too large for a 3-quart pot. Follow your model manual if it requires more liquid. Never force an overfilled batch into the cooker.
Use thawed meat. Cut and measure the ingredients as listed before starting. In these steps, "pot" means the removable metal pot inside the Instant Pot. Keep the lid off except during the pressure-cooking steps. Wash hands and tools after handling raw meat; use a clean plate for cooked meat.
Allow extra time for preparation, pressure to build (about 15–25 minutes), and finishing the sauce.

1). With the lid off, select Sauté. Add the oil, onion, and bell peppers. Cook for 6 minutes, stirring often. Add the garlic and stir for 30 seconds. Press Cancel.

2). Pour in the broth. Scrape the bottom clean with a wooden spoon. Stir in the paprika, caraway seeds, salt, and black pepper.

3). Add the beef cubes. Spoon the tomato paste and diced tomatoes with their juice over the meat. Leave the tomatoes on top without stirring.

4). Lock the lid. Set the steam control to Sealing if your model has that switch. Select Pressure Cook / High for 35 minutes. Press Start if needed. The cooking countdown begins after pressure builds.

5). When cooking ends, leave the lid closed for 15 minutes. Then use your model's steam-release control. Keep your hands and face away from the steam. If liquid sprays, stop releasing and wait longer.

6). Wait until all steam stops and the pressure pin drops. Open the lid away from your face. Never force the lid open.

7). Test a beef cube with a fork. If it is not tender, select Sauté / Low and simmer uncovered for 15–30 minutes, stirring. Add up to 1/2 cup water if the sauce gets too thick. Continue until the beef is easy to bite.

8). Mix the cornstarch with 2 tablespoons cold water in a small bowl until smooth.

9). Select Sauté / Low. Stir the cornstarch mixture into the bubbling stew. Stir gently for 2–3 minutes until the broth thickens. Press Cancel.

10). Divide the stew among 7 shallow containers. Divide the sour cream evenly into 7 small separate containers; add it after reheating each meal. Keep the sour cream refrigerated and use according to its label. Follow the storage directions below.

Check doneness: Whole-cut beef and pork must reach at least 145°F and rest 3 minutes before eating. These shoulder and chuck recipes need longer cooking until fork-tender; do not stop at the minimum temperature if the meat is still tough.
Store your 7 meals: Refrigerate in shallow containers within 2 hours (1 hour if the room is above 90°F). Keep the fridge at 40°F or colder. Keep the first 3 portions in the fridge and freeze the other 4 on cooking day. Use refrigerated leftovers within 3–4 days. Thaw frozen portions in the fridge.
Reheat: Heat each meal to 165°F throughout. In the microwave, use a vented cover, stir halfway through, let it stand briefly, and check several spots with a thermometer. Add a little water if needed.
Recipe developed for this collection; not kitchen-tested. Food safety: safe cooking temperatures, leftover storage, reheating. Cooker guidance: Instant Pot.
