Chipotle Beef Barbacoa
High pressure: 60 minutes. Natural release: 20 minutes, then carefully vent the remainder.
Approximate total time: 1 hr 50 min–2 hr 15 min. All meat must be thawed.
Use a 6- or 8-quart pot. If the load exceeds PC MAX, split the ingredients into two batches and use a full 540 ml broth in EACH batch, with the same pressure time. Buy extra broth for that split-batch option. Never exceed PC MAX / 2/3. Follow your model manual for its liquid minimum and release controls. Never force the lid; open only after the float drops.

1). Brown in batches
Pat the thawed beef dry. Season with the measured salt and pepper. Select Sauté / High and add the oil. Brown two or three batches, about 2 minutes per broad side; transfer to a plate. Browning adds flavor; the centers remain raw.

2). Build the braising liquid
Sauté the onion for 3 minutes, then garlic for 30 seconds. Press Cancel. Pour in the full 540 ml broth and scrape the bottom completely clean. Add vinegar, chipotles with adobo, cumin, oregano and bay leaves. Return beef and its juices. Keep the load below the PC MAX line.

3). Pressure cook
Lock the lid and set the valve to Sealing, if your model requires it. Select Pressure Cook / High for 60 minutes. Allow roughly 15–25 minutes to come to pressure before the countdown begins.

4). Release and check
After the cycle, leave the valve alone for a 20-minute natural release. Then carefully vent any remaining pressure. Open only after the float valve drops. The beef should pull apart easily. If still firm, keep it in the liquid, cook another 10 minutes on High, and naturally release for 10 minutes before venting.

5). Shred and concentrate
Remove the bay leaves. Transfer beef to a clean tray and shred. With the lid off, simmer the juices on Sauté for 10–15 minutes, stirring, until slightly reduced. Turn off the heat, add lime juice, and return the beef. The sauce remains a braising juice, not a thick gravy.

6). Make seven portions
Mix the meat and juices well, then divide evenly among seven shallow containers. For precise portions, weigh the finished food without the pot and divide that weight by seven. Cool and store promptly as described below.

Food safety
Whole-cut beef has a minimum safe temperature of 145°F (63°C) followed by a 3-minute rest. Chuck needs much longer cooking to shred; check both temperature and fork-tenderness.
Wash hands and tools after handling raw meat; keep cooked food off raw-meat plates. Do not rinse raw poultry.

Meal-prep storage
Divide promptly into shallow containers; refrigerate within 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F / 32°C). Do not leave the full pot out to cool. Keep the fridge at 40°F / 4°C or colder. Keep portions 1-3 refrigerated and freeze portions 4-7 on cooking day. Use refrigerated leftovers within 3-4 days. Freeze at 0°F / -18°C or colder.

Freezing and reheating
Freeze meat with its juices. Best texture within 3 months. Thaw one portion overnight in the refrigerator; reheat covered with 1–2 tbsp water or broth, stirring to break up cold pockets.
Reheat to 165°F / 74°C throughout. In a microwave, cover with a vented lid, stir or turn, let stand briefly and check several spots with a thermometer. Do not thaw on the counter or use Keep Warm to reheat. Freezer times above are quality targets.

Recipe nutrition estimate
About 620 kcal and 51 g protein per one-seventh of the finished recipe, including sauce, excluding sides. Allow roughly 20% variation in calories and 15% in protein, sometimes more for fatty meat. These recipe estimates use generic ingredients; your app calculates from its item records, which can differ. The estimate counts all beef fat, oil and juices as eaten. Trimming or discarding rendered fat can lower calories substantially.
Optional serving suggestion: For a rice bowl, add 1 cup cooked rice per serving (7 cups for the batch): about +205 kcal and +4 g protein. With the barbacoa, that is roughly 825 kcal per bowl. Your keto tortillas also work; count their package-label nutrition separately.
