Steps:
Pork Carnitas
High pressure: 60 minutes. Wait after cooking: 20 minutes with the lid closed, then release the remaining steam as explained below.
Approximate total time: 1 hr 45 min–2 hr 10 min. All meat must be thawed.
Use a 6- or 8-quart Instant Pot. Stay below the line marked PC MAX inside the metal pot, about two-thirds full. The amounts in the steps are for one full batch. If the full recipe will not fit, cook two half batches. Use half of each amount listed in the steps, except the broth added before pressure cooking: use 2¼ cups of that broth in EACH batch. Keep the pressure-cooking time the same. Buy extra broth for two batches. A 3-quart pot is too small for these directions.
Never fill above the PC MAX line. Follow your model manual for its minimum liquid and its steam-release controls.
In these directions, "pot" means the removable metal pot inside the Instant Pot. Keep the lid off until a step tells you to close it. Have a food thermometer, a large spoon, tongs, and a clean plate or bowl ready. Use thawed meat. Chop or slice the ingredients as listed before turning on the pot.

1). Get the pork ready
Use 4 pounds of thawed, boneless pork shoulder after trimming off the skin and thick outer layer of fat. Cut the meat into chunks about 2 inches across.

2). Pour the liquids into the pot
Pour 2¼ cups chicken broth, ½ cup orange juice, and ¼ cup lime juice into the Instant Pot.

3). Add the onion, garlic, and seasonings
Add the diced onion, 6 finely chopped garlic cloves, 1 tablespoon cumin, 2 teaspoons oregano, 1 teaspoon salt, 1 teaspoon black pepper, and 2 bay leaves to the liquids in the pot. Stir.

4). Add the raw pork
Put all the pork chunks into the Instant Pot. Stir so the liquid coats the meat. Stay below the pressure-cooking maximum line. You do not need to brown the pork first.

5). Close the lid
Put the lid on the Instant Pot and turn it until it locks. If your model has a Sealing/Venting switch, move it to Sealing. Follow your model's instructions if its controls look different.

6). Cook on High pressure for 60 minutes
Press Pressure Cook. Choose High pressure and set the cooking time to 60 minutes. Press Start if your model requires it. The pot may take 15–25 minutes to build pressure before the cooking countdown starts.

7). Wait 20 minutes after cooking ends
When pressure cooking finishes, start a separate 20-minute timer. Leave the lid closed and do not touch the steam-release control during this wait. This lets some pressure drop by itself.

8). Let the remaining steam out
After the waiting time, use your model's steam-release control to release the remaining pressure. Keep your face and hands away from the steam. If liquid sprays out, stop releasing steam and wait longer before trying again.

9). Open the lid
Wait until steam stops coming out and the small pressure pin has dropped. Unlock the lid and lift it so the steam goes away from your face. Never force the lid open.

10). Check whether the pork pulls apart
Try pulling a pork chunk apart with two forks. It should come apart easily. If it is still firm, leave it in the liquid, pressure cook for another 10 minutes on High, wait 10 minutes, then release the remaining steam and open the lid. Check again.

11). Remove the bay leaves and pork
Find and throw away both bay leaves. Use tongs to move the cooked pork from the Instant Pot to a clean, large bowl. Leave the cooking liquid in the pot.

12). Shred the pork in the bowl
Use two forks to pull the cooked pork into small strips. Remove large pieces of visible fat if you do not want to eat them.

13). Add cooking juice to the shredded pork
Measure 1½ cups of liquid from the Instant Pot. Pour it over the shredded pork in the bowl and stir. Keep the remaining cooking liquid nearby. If the meat looks dry, add more of that liquid a little at a time.

14). Optional: spread the pork on baking pans
For crispy pork today, spread the shredded pork on two metal baking pans that are safe under a broiler. Drizzle 1 tablespoon olive oil across the pork on both pans. Do not use parchment paper. For meal prep, you can skip this step and the next step; save the oil for reheating day.

15). Optional: brown the edges under the broiler
Follow your oven's broiler instructions. Put one pan at a time about 6 inches below the broiler. Cook for 4–8 minutes, watching the whole time, until the edges brown. Use oven mitts to remove the hot pan.

16). Fill seven containers
Divide the pork and its juices evenly among seven shallow containers. Include any juices left in the bowl or on the baking pans. Follow the cooling and storage directions below right away. Pork stored without the broiler step can be crisped when you reheat it.

17). When reheating a portion
Heat the pork and its juice to 165°F first. For crispy edges, spread the hot pork in a broiler-safe pan and broil briefly while watching. If you saved the oil, use a little less than ½ teaspoon per portion so the original tablespoon lasts all seven portions.

Food safety
Whole-cut pork has a minimum safe temperature of 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest. Shoulder must also be cooked until fork-tender to make carnitas.
Wash hands and tools after handling raw meat; keep cooked food off raw-meat plates. Do not rinse raw poultry.

Meal-prep storage
Divide promptly into shallow containers; refrigerate within 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F / 32°C). Do not leave the full pot out to cool. Keep the fridge at 40°F / 4°C or colder. Keep portions 1-3 refrigerated and freeze portions 4-7 on cooking day. Use refrigerated leftovers within 3-4 days. Freeze at 0°F / -18°C or colder.

Freezing and reheating
Freeze the shredded pork with juice, preferably before crisping. Best texture within 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator. Reheat covered with a splash of water until 165°F throughout; then crisp in a skillet or under the broiler.
Reheat to 165°F / 74°C throughout. In a microwave, cover with a vented lid, stir or turn, let stand briefly and check several spots with a thermometer. Do not thaw on the counter or use Keep Warm to reheat. Freezer times above are quality targets.
