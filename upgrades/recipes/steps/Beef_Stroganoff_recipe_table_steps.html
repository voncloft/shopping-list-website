Beef Stroganoff
High pressure: 35 minutes. Natural release: 15 minutes, then carefully vent the remainder.
Approximate total time: 1 hr 35 min–1 hr 55 min. All meat must be thawed.
Use a 6- or 8-quart pot. If the load exceeds PC MAX, split the ingredients into two batches and use a full 540 ml broth in EACH batch, with the same pressure time. Buy extra broth for that split-batch option. Never exceed PC MAX / 2/3. Follow your model manual for its liquid minimum and release controls. Never force the lid; open only after the float drops.

1). Cook the mushrooms
Select Sauté / High. Melt the butter and cook mushrooms in two batches, about 5–6 minutes each, until their liquid mostly evaporates. Transfer to a clean bowl. They go back in after pressure cooking.

2). Brown the beef
Add oil to the pot. Season thawed beef with salt and pepper. Brown in three batches, about 2 minutes per broad side, transferring each batch to a plate. Add onion and cook for 3 minutes; add garlic for 30 seconds.

3). Deglaze and load
Press Cancel. Add all 540 ml broth and scrape the bottom until no browned bits are stuck. Stir in Worcestershire and Dijon, then return beef and its juices. Check PC MAX. Do not add mushrooms, cornstarch, sour cream or noodles yet.

4). Pressure and release
Lock the lid and set Sealing if needed. Cook on High pressure for 35 minutes, allowing about 15–25 minutes to pressurize. Naturally release for 15 minutes, then vent the remainder carefully. Open only after the float drops. If beef is not fork-tender, cook another 5–10 minutes on High in the liquid and naturally release for 10 minutes.

5). Thicken the sauce
Stir the cooked mushrooms into the beef. Select Sauté / Normal and bring to a simmer. Re-stir the cornstarch-water slurry, pour it in, and stir for 2–3 minutes until thickened. Press Cancel.

6). Temper the sour cream
Let bubbling stop. Whisk about ½ cup of hot sauce gradually into the sour cream in a bowl, then fold it back into the pot. Warm gently if necessary; do not boil hard. Divide beef, mushrooms and sauce evenly into seven portions. Keep any noodles separate.

Food safety
Whole-cut beef must reach at least 145°F (63°C) with a 3-minute rest. Chuck should also be fork-tender; tough beef needs more cooking even when it is already safe.
Wash hands and tools after handling raw meat; keep cooked food off raw-meat plates. Do not rinse raw poultry.

Meal-prep storage
Divide promptly into shallow containers; refrigerate within 2 hours (1 hour above 90°F / 32°C). Do not leave the full pot out to cool. Keep the fridge at 40°F / 4°C or colder. Keep portions 1-3 refrigerated and freeze portions 4-7 on cooking day. Use refrigerated leftovers within 3-4 days. Freeze at 0°F / -18°C or colder.

Freezing and reheating
Freeze the beef and sauce without noodles; best texture within 2 months. Sour cream may become a little grainy after thawing. Thaw in the refrigerator and reheat gently, stirring, until 165°F. Add 1–2 tbsp water if the gravy is too thick.
Reheat to 165°F / 74°C throughout. In a microwave, cover with a vented lid, stir or turn, let stand briefly and check several spots with a thermometer. Do not thaw on the counter or use Keep Warm to reheat. Freezer times above are quality targets.

Recipe nutrition estimate
About 780 kcal and 55 g protein per one-seventh of the finished recipe, including sauce, excluding sides. Allow roughly 20% variation in calories and 15% in protein, sometimes more for fatty meat. These recipe estimates use generic ingredients; your app calculates from its item records, which can differ. Base nutrition includes meat, mushrooms, gravy and sour cream; it excludes noodles. All cooking oil, butter and beef fat are counted.
Optional serving suggestion: Cook 14 oz (397 g) dry egg noodles separately for the batch: 2 oz (57 g) dry per serving, about +210 kcal and +7 g protein. With stroganoff, that is roughly 990 kcal per serving. Follow the noodle package cooking directions.
